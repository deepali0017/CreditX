Government Guidelines

Regulatory frameworks for carbon credits and energy efficiency in India

🏛️
Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) — Ministry of Power, Government of India. Legally binding for Designated Consumers (DCs) under the Energy Conservation Act, 2001.
SEC Target Compliance
Designated Consumers must achieve their Specific Energy Consumption (SEC) targets within the PAT cycle period. Failure requires purchasing ESCerts or paying penalties up to ₹10 lakh per day.
Source: BEE Circular No. 31/2/2012-BEE · Updated 2023
Mandatory Energy Audits
All Designated Consumers must conduct mandatory energy audits every 3 years by a BEE-accredited energy auditor. Results must be submitted on the BEE portal within 60 days.
Source: Energy Conservation Act 2001, Section 14 · Rule 8
⚠️ Penalty Structure (2024)
Non-compliance: ₹10 lakh first offence, ₹10 lakh + ₹1,000/day continuing. Failure to designate Energy Manager: ₹5 lakh. Failure to submit annual returns: ₹1 lakh.
Source: Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act 2022
ℹ️ 2024 Amendment — Carbon Credits
The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act 2022 empowers the Central Government to specify carbon credits. Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs) are now tradeable on government-designated exchanges under CCTS 2023.
Source: Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act 2022 · CCTS Rules 2023
🌿
Perform, Achieve & Trade (PAT) Scheme — India's flagship market-based mechanism to enhance energy efficiency in energy-intensive industries.
PAT Cycle VI (2023–2026)
Current cycle covers 13 sectors: Aluminium, Cement, Chlor-Alkali, Fertilizer, Iron & Steel, Paper & Pulp, Petrochemical, Petroleum Refinery, Pharmaceutical, Textile, Railways, Municipal Corporation, and Thermal Power Plants.
Source: MoP Notification · PAT Cycle VI Launch 2023
ESCert Trading Rules
1 ESCert = 1 Metric Tonne of Oil Equivalent (MTOE) saved. Tradeable on IEX and PXIL. Over-achievers earn ESCerts; under-achievers must buy or pay penalty.
Source: PAT Trading Guidelines 2020
📋
Energy Savings Certificate (ESCert) Rules — Governing framework for certificate issuance and trading under EC Act 2001.
ESCert Issuance Process
Step 1: Submit APR to BEE by June 30. Step 2: BEE-empanelled agency verifies. Step 3: ESCerts issued within 90 days. Step 4: Credits appear in DC account for trading.
Source: BEE ESCert Procedure Manual 2019
⚠️ APR Submission Deadline
Annual Performance Report must be submitted by June 30 each year. Late submission: ₹10,000/day penalty. Three consecutive failures may trigger enhanced compliance review.
Source: EC Act 2001 · BEE Circular 2024
🪙
Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) 2023 — India's national carbon market framework.
Carbon Credit Certificate (CCC)
1 CCC = 1 tonne of CO₂ equivalent reduced below baseline. Issued by BEE after third-party verification. Tradeable on IEX and PXIL.
Source: CCTS Rules 2023 · Ministry of Power Notification
ℹ️ International Carbon Market Linkage
Under Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement, India has bilateral agreements with Singapore, Sweden, and UAE for Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes (ITMOs). Indian industries can export ITMOs at $15–45/tonne vs ₹600–900 domestic.
Source: MoEFCC Article 6 Policy 2024
